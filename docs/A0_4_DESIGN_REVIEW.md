## Power and fault paths

R50 connects AVDD to ground and R51 connects DVDD to ground. Each is 1 kΩ ±1%, 0.125 W, beside its regulator output capacitor. The traces connect directly to the capacitor pads. Their purpose is to drain stored rail charge and sink a small DC injection even when the normal load is off. They add approximately 8.3 mA at nominal voltage. At the high supply corners the individual loads are at most 5.15 and 3.40 mA. Dissipation is at most 26.3 and 11.5 mW respectively. [Yageo part data](https://www.yageogroup.com/component-documentation/download/specsheet/RC0805FR-071KL)

The analog/digital branch allowances are now 36/14 mA including these loads. At a 7.2 V pack, 50 mA through a 15 Ω PTC plus 0.5 V across D10 and 0.1 V wiring/switch drop leaves 5.85 V at the regulators. That leaves 0.50 V beyond 5.10 V plus 0.25 V dropout. At the opposite pack corner the upper regulator dissipation estimates are 0.191 W and 0.098 W. These margins depend on the assumed currents, warm PTC resistance, pack droop and enclosure temperature. Keep the Nano on its own fused branch. Measure both regulator inputs/outputs and Nano VIN during radio bursts near the low-pack limit. Retain D10 upstream to inhibit reverse flow toward the pack. Both LDOs share its downstream input node. [TI TPS7A24 §8.1.4–5](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps7a24.pdf)

With all sources removed and only direct rail capacitors in the model, R50/R51 give upper RC time constants of 26.6/8.94 ms under the stated capacitance allowances. The ADC's internal reservoirs, regulator paths, connector sources and sequencing can change the decay. Firmware must reinitialize after invalid rails and observe both the power-on interval and VCAP1 readiness. Use an oscilloscope to establish timing on the assembled board. [TI ADS1299 §11.1](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ads1299.pdf)

## Signal-path decisions

Retain the two 10 kΩ resistors and 1 nF C0G shunt on each recording/reference path for this measurement revision. The shared reference drives eight negative inputs. Increasing the resistors to limit fault current trades away noise and interference rejection. For example, 100 kΩ per resistor would reduce the 5.1 V intact single-path bound tenfold, increase resistor noise by √10, and move the nominal input pole from 7.96 kHz to 796 Hz. With the same illustrative 140 pF shared-versus-single capacitance difference, common-mode conversion at 50 Hz grows roughly tenfold. A patient-protection solution must account for these tradeoffs. Reassess the input network or a buffered reference against measured noise, imbalance and fault criteria.

Retain both bulk reference capacitors and the bias feedback network. Test all eight channels with a dummy differential source, aiming for ≤1 µV RMS over 0.5–40 Hz and ≥60 dB common-mode rejection on the balanced fixture. Sweep electrode impedance, cable capacitance and bias masks. Require repeatable startup, stable bias and recovery before enabling closed-loop bias for external measurements. The [verification plan](reviews/independent-577aad8-2026-11-17/VERIFICATION_PLAN.md) gives the fixtures and measurement sequence.

## What the fabrication data demonstrate

KiCad 9.0.2 passed ERC, full schematic-parity DRC, all 581 netlist checks, 448 footprint/pad checks and five routing checks. The five formerly ignored DRC categories were enabled. Exact project footprint IDs were added to the relevant symbol filters, clearing the previous 13 metadata warnings. Nine workflow tests passed. The 14 electrical calculations are conditional models. The assembly has 109 electrical parts, four mounting holes, 1,185 routed segments, 123 vias and two filled inner-layer zones.

The [fabrication directory](../hardware/fabrication/A0.4/) contains a bare-board ZIP and separate hand-assembly references. The CAM verifier reads the exported Gerbers and Excellon files independently of KiCad. The archive is prepared for engineering and fabricator review. Physical fit, manufacturer tolerances, analog performance and system safety remain separate open decisions.
